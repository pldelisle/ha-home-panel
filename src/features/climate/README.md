# Climate

What the Climate tab computes and draws ([spec 20](../../../docs/specs/20-climate.md)): the Hilo thermostats and the heat pump as a list, the phone's dial and the tablet's gauge. This folder holds the pure half of the first move: the heat pump's reading, the list's items, and the dial and the gauge as geometry (#242), and the model that words and shapes all of it (#243); and the pane, the list, the detail, the gauge and the dial that draw it (#244). The history's rules come as pure functions (#245) and are loaded and drawn in the move after it (#246): the pane loads one range at a time through the panel's cache and draws the chart and the heatmap through `<hp-chart>`. The controls, the draggable dial and the commands (#248) end the move: the legacy panel keeps no Climate code but its Home tile's line and the gate that hides the tile.

## The heat pump

`heatPump` reads the config's `heat_pump` entity: the mode (its state), the action, the temperature, the target, the range (`min_temp ?? 16`, `max_temp ?? 30`) and the lists of modes, fan, preset and louvers. The **brand** is `Fujitsu` when the entity's platform is FGLair or its device's manufacturer says Fujitsu, else the manufacturer, else `null`: the provider words nothing, and the list calls an unnamed maker "Heat pump". An unavailable unit reads the defaults, and a configured entity Home Assistant does not know is `present: false`, which the list words "Not found".

## The geometry

`dialGeometry` and `gaugeGeometry` return numbers and path data, never markup: the element places them with `svg` templates, as Energy's `flowLayout` does, so the hardest SVG in the panel is tested with numbers. The dial's setpoint has three states, kept apart: a number (on, or off at the 5 degree frost guard), `null` (off) and `undefined` (not loaded, as on the house's dial, which has no knob). The words ("Off", the gauge's caption) are the model's, from `off` and `caption`. `dialValueFromPointer` is the way back: the angle from the dial's centre, snapped to half degrees, with `'off'` at or below the frost guard (the legacy returned the lowest step `lo` there, so the command that follows, #243 and #248, sends `lo` for `'off'`), and the quarter turn below the dial read as the nearer end.

## The model

`climateModel` takes the inputs, the range's loaded history (`ClimateHistoryData`, `null` before the load: the Range and Heating tiles show a dash and the three charts' places say "Loading history…", or that it failed when `historyFailed`), the selection (`house`, `hp` or a room's key; one that names nothing the house has shows the house, and `selection` says which) and the deps: the catalog and formats, the overlay, `compact` (the phone: the dial, not the big number and the gauge), the range, and the element state, the "set every room" value (the house's `asks.set` takes it). It returns the strings and shapes the elements place, and the controls as a discriminated union (`hp`, `house`, `room`, each possibly `offline`) the element switches on and never computes. A held setpoint or target (`K.sp`, `K.hp.sp`) wins in the dial and the controls and not in the pill or the gauge, which follow the unit, as the legacy panel did; the heat pump's held mode is read once and decides the pill, the gauge's mark and the dial together. The frost guard's rule (below 5.5 is off) is read, never applied: the commands that follow it are `commands.ts`'s.

## The history

`history.ts` is `loadClimRange` and the chart code of the legacy panel as functions over statistics rows, so each rule is named and pinned with literal rows; the loader asks the shared cache and these derive what it brings back (`deriveClimateHistory`), and `ClimateCharts` draws what the options builders return. A row is drawn at the middle of its bucket (`RANGES`' `bucketMs`, halved), to two decimals. **The setpoint rule**: a stretch off throughout (its highest setpoint at or below the frost guard's 5.5) is a gap, one never off shows its mean, one partly off shows its highest. **Heating** is each period's mean kW times its hours, the running period only the hours so far. **The house** is the mean of the rooms at each instant every room has a reading for. **When the heating ran** is a muted step area on a second axis at the right of the room, house or heat pump chart (not columns: ApexCharts never draws a column wider than the smallest gap between any two points of any series, and the heat pump's line has points a millisecond apart). A room's steps are its power sensor's mean kW per bucket (`heatingPower`; a bucket at or under `HEATING_KW`, 50 W, is a 0, which draws nothing), the house's their sum (`sumHeating`; a room with no row at a bucket counts 0), and the heat pump's, which has no power sensor, the whole percent of each bucket its state history spent `heating` (`heatingShare`: a point holds until the next, an `unavailable` one does not count, the running bucket counts only up to the load's instant). A step sits at its bucket's start, because a step line holds a value until the next point, and one closing point at the last bucket's end (or the load's instant) gives the last step its width. For a room and the house `heatingAlong` gives one step for each point of the line, at the same position in the list, because ApexCharts' shared tooltip reads every series by position, not by time; the heat pump's irregular line cannot line up that way. The kW axis runs from 0 to the largest step in whole kW, never under 1, the share's from 0 to 100 %, and the temperature lines keep one shared left scale; with nothing heated the options are the line chart's alone. **`bucket`** groups a series into the heatmap's cells: the local hour (a day), the six-hour block (a week), the local day (a month) or the week, which starts on Monday by arithmetic on the day key so it does not move with the zone (a year); every cell has a unique key and label, and an `axis` text that says what the heatmap's axis writes under it. The words of a week's blocks are `climate.block.*`; a day's label is the weekday and the hour as `fmt` writes them. The colours are the heat scale's `[degrees, colour]` stops blended in OKLab (`src/core/color.ts`: perceptually even, so neighbouring cells join without a band), in 0.1 degree bands for the chart. The three options builders return plain data (ADR 0022) with the page's colours as tokens the caller gives; the room chart's curve is straight for the heat pump, `monotoneCubic` with a setpoint (ApexCharts splits a `smooth` line once any series has a gap) and `smooth` otherwise. The titles by range (`RANGE_WORDS`) are Climate's own table and not the kit's `RANGES`, which holds only what every reader of a range needs.

## The loader and the charts

`ClimateHistory` (`loader.ts`) is a reactive controller the pane mounts, after Energy's: while the detail is on screen (the tablet's tab, or the phone's sheet; never for the phone's tiles, never on Home) it keeps one entry per range, asks the cache for the range's window (`climateWindow`: one statistics request, and the heat pump's state history when the house has one) and draws an entry from memory for ten minutes. The window starts at the top of the hour, so the shared cache answers a second request within the hour and the end-to-end clock, which sits on the hour, draws what the legacy panel drew. A reply for a range or config that is no longer asked for, or that arrives after the pane left, is dropped; a `StaleReply` is no failure; a failure is logged, said by the charts, and asked again no sooner than a minute later, for its own range only. `ClimateCharts` (`charts.ts`) turns the history into the three charts' options and hands back the same objects while the history, the selection, the theme and the language are the same, so `<hp-chart>` draws nothing again for a thermostat's new state, and the heatmap is not drawn again when another room is chosen. The page's colours are read only when a chart is built. The heat pump's history that fails to read is an empty line, and the rooms still draw; its note says where the line starts when the recorder began it more than a day after the range (`lateHeatPumpStart`).

## Words

The words are `climate.*` messages through the `t` the caller gives; the heat pump's own (modes, fan speeds, presets and louver positions) are the catalog's where Home Assistant's name is known and are shown capitalised, with the underscore a space, where it is not. The rules are the legacy panel's own, copied as they were, constants included; the legacy panel's copies went with #244 to #248.

## The pane and the controls

`<hp-climate-pane>` keeps the room and the range for the panel's life, reads the held values from the panel's overlay (`OptimisticController`) for the model, and draws the header, the phone's tiles and sheet, the tablet's list and detail, and the heatmap under them. The chart is the detail's (`<hp-chart>` under the tiles, with its legend); the heatmap (a title, `<hp-heat-legend>` and the chart) is the pane's block under the grid, on a tablet only (the outside is the room chart's line, so it has no chart of its own since 2026-11-17). On a phone a tile asks for `climate.detail` with its room and the sheet holds the same detail with the dial; Home's Climate tile asks for it with nothing and gets the house.

## The commands and the controls

A command is data (`commands.ts`): the service, the keys it holds in the overlay and the toast it says when it is done, as a catalog key; it words nothing, so the formatters and the word of a mode, a fan or a louver position come in as arguments. `nextSetpoint(current, delta, lo, hi)` is the stepper's rule, pure and tested: below 5.5 going down is the lowest setpoint, the thermostat off; up from off is 15; everything on the half degrees and inside the thermostat's range. `<hp-climate-controls>` and the detail's dial send through `ControlSender`: a tap shows its value at once, a setpoint is sent 1,100 ms after the last tap (five taps are one `climate.set_temperature`, a drag of the dial is one), a mode, a fan, a preset or a louver position now; a host that leaves the document sends nothing and lets go of the values it was holding. "Set all" and "All off" ask first, in the kit's `<hp-dialog>` (#378): the model words both questions (`asks`: Set all's title and line take the stepper's value, All off's line says the 5° frost guard), the element holds which one is open (`asking`), and the dialog's confirm sends one call with every thermostat's id; Cancel, Escape and the backdrop send nothing. On a phone the dial is the setpoint and the heat pump's target, so their steppers are not drawn there; the quick buttons, the groups and "set every room" are. The dial's drag is held in the overlay, so a thermostat reporting another setpoint meanwhile does not move the knob under the finger.
